Why this model exists

It answers the packing question

A 66 mm cylinder sounds generous until you put a camera, a sensor board, a microcontroller, a battery and a radio in it. Everything is at real size, so a part that does not fit is visibly not fitting.

It shows the parachute problem

Switch to Deployed. The canopy is 0.58 m across — 8.8× the width of the can. Teams routinely underestimate how much fabric that is and how long the shroud lines have to be.

It is a scored deliverable

The guidelines reward "an enhanced 3D CanSat model" as a kit enhancement. This is that — plus two files you can hand straight to a 3D printer.

Component masses are a preliminary budget, not weighed hardware: they total 350 g, the same figure the flight simulation uses, so the parachute sizing here matches the parachute sizing in the mission. Replace them with real measurements as soon as the kit arrives — this model is a spreadsheet you can look at.

Print files

Generated from the same dimensions as the model above, as ASCII STL — a hollow shell and a lid, for a fit check before you cut anything real:

  • cansat_shell.stl — Ø66 × 115 mm, 1.2 mm wall, closed base, open top
  • cansat_lid.stl — top cap with a centred antenna hole; cut the camera window to suit your module
